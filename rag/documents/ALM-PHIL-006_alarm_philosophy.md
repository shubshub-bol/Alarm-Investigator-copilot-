# Alarm Management Philosophy and Response Priorities

## 1. Purpose

This document sets how alarms are prioritized, acknowledged and maintained across all sites. It follows the
principles of ISA-18.2 / IEC 62682.

## 2. Priority definitions

| Priority | Meaning | Response required within |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Immediate threat to safety, environment or a major unit trip | 5 minutes |
| P2 | Urgent: equipment damage or production loss likely | 15 minutes |
| P3 | Planned: needs action this shift | End of shift |
| P4 | Low: information or maintenance follow-up | Next working day |

## 3. Acknowledgement

Operators acknowledge alarms within 10 minutes. Acknowledging is not resolving: an acknowledged alarm still
needs the response in its procedure.

## 4. Alarm floods

A flood is more than 10 alarms in 10 minutes for one operator. During a flood, find the initiating (first)
alarm and respond to it before working through the consequential alarms. Review every flood afterwards.

## 5. Stale, chattering and nuisance alarms

- A stale alarm has been standing for more than 24 hours.
- A chattering alarm clears and returns repeatedly within minutes.
- Both are reviewed weekly by the alarm management team. Suppression or shelving needs a management of change
  (MOC) approval. Safety alarms are never suppressed.

## 6. Escalation

Inform the shift supervisor of every P1 alarm and of any P2 alarm not resolved within 30 minutes.

## 7. Use of decision-support recommendations

Recommendations from decision-support tools, analytics or AI assistants are advisory. Where a recommendation
conflicts with an approved operating procedure, the approved procedure governs and the conflict must be
reported to the procedure owner.
