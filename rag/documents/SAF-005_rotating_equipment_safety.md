# Rotating Equipment Safety and Isolation Instructions

## 1. Mandatory rules

- Never touch, open or inspect rotating equipment that has not been isolated and proven dead.
- Every inspection beyond a visual walk-by requires a permit to work.
- Alarm troubleshooting from the control room is not a reason to skip field isolation.

## 2. Lockout and tagout (LOTO) for a pump and its motor

1. Stop the pump from the DCS and confirm it has stopped.
2. Open and lock the motor circuit breaker; apply a personal lock and tag.
3. Close and lock the suction and discharge isolation valves.
4. Depressurize and drain the pump casing to the closed drain system.
5. Verify zero energy: attempt a local start (try-start) and confirm the motor does not run.
6. Only then remove the coupling guard or open bearing housings.

## 3. Hot and high-pressure fluids

Boiler feedwater can be above 150 degC and 150 bar. Pump casings, bearing housings and lube oil can exceed
60 degC after shutdown. Allow cooling time and use heat-resistant gloves.

## 4. Personal protective equipment

Hard hat, safety glasses, hearing protection near running machines, flame-resistant clothing and safety
boots. Use a face shield when draining pump casings.

## 5. Permit to work

Mechanical work on feed pumps and compressors needs a permit signed by the area authority. The permit lists
the isolation points, the gas test result (for compressors) and the people working under it.

## 6. Stop-work authority

Anyone may stop a job that looks unsafe. No procedure, instruction or recommendation overrides stop-work
authority.
